# Research Playbook: Sweeping an Area and Reading a Business From the Outside

This is the how of prospect research: where to look, what a signal looks like from public
information, and how to score a business you have never spoken to.

---

## Part 1: The Area Sweep

### Sources, in order of usefulness

| Source | What it gives you | How to use it |
|--------|-------------------|---------------|
| Map and business listing platforms | The raw universe: who exists in the area, category, reviews, hours | Search "[vertical] in [town]" per target vertical; note review count and recency as a proxy for size and activity |
| Professional network company pages | Employee count (the single best public headcount signal), staff titles, hiring activity | Search the company; check its people listing for IT titles and the count trend |
| Chamber of commerce and local business directories (the chambers for the towns in {{SERVICE_AREA}}, plus county business associations) | Established, community-engaged businesses; these convert well for a local trust business | Sweep member directories in the target vertical |
| Industry association directories (state bar, CPA societies, medical/dental directories, contractor licensing) | Vertical-specific lists with locations, often practice size hints | Use when the run has a vertical focus |
| Your state's Secretary of State business registry | Legal name, standing, registration date (business age), registered agent | Verify a candidate is real, active, and locally registered |
| Local business press (regional business journals, local papers, chamber newsletters) | Growth stories, expansions, moves, awards | Search "[town] business expansion/new office/award" for the past year |
| Job boards | Hiring = growth = the "we hired five people and things are falling apart" moment. Also reveals whether they're hiring IT staff (which removes them from the ICP) | Search jobs posted by companies in the area |

### Sweep discipline

- Target 15-25 candidates for a default run. Below 10, widen the net (more verticals or a
  larger radius). Above 30, tighten to the best-scoring verticals before deep research.
- Prioritize verticals in msp-sales ICP order: manufacturing, healthcare/dental/medical,
  accounting/CPA, legal, finance/wealth management, construction, then general professional
  services.
- Skip: national chains and franchises with corporate IT, businesses under ~8 employees,
  government and schools, other IT providers, and businesses that appear closed or dormant.
- Record the source of each candidate as you go; the spreadsheet has a column for it.

## Part 2: Reading a Business From the Outside

You cannot ask discovery questions yet, so you infer. Here is what each ICP signal looks like
from public information. Never present an inference as a fact; the dossier labels each as
observed or estimated.

### Headcount (ICP: 10-100, sweet spot 25-75)

- The professional network's employee count is the anchor, but it undercounts field-heavy
  businesses (construction crews and manufacturing floor staff rarely have profiles). For
  those, multiply the listed number by 2-3x or use fleet size, location count, or "our team"
  pages.
- Website team pages, number of locations, size of office (street view), review volume all
  triangulate.

### No dedicated IT staff (ICP: high weight)

- Search the professional network for people at the company with IT titles (IT manager,
  sysadmin, IT director). Finding one is close to disqualifying; finding none at a 30-person
  company is a strong positive signal.
- Job postings for IT roles mean they are solving the problem in-house: usually a red flag,
  though a stale unfilled IT posting can mean they are struggling to hire and open to
  alternatives. Note which it is.

### Compliance pressure (ICP: very high weight)

- Inferred from vertical: healthcare/dental (HIPAA), legal (bar confidentiality), CPA and
  finance (IRS/SEC/FINRA, cyber insurance), anyone taking cards (PCI), government
  contractors (CMMC). If the vertical implies it, the signal counts at desk stage.

### Growth (ICP: medium weight)

- Active job postings, a recent move or second location, press coverage, "now hiring" on the
  website, headcount trend on the professional network if visible.

### IT pain clues (bonus signals, not in the formal ICP but worth recording)

- Dated or broken website (copyright year years old, broken links, no HTTPS): weak but real
  proxy for "nobody owns technology here."
- Reviews mentioning phones down, online booking broken, "couldn't process my card."
- Free consumer email domain (a free mailbox provider instead of their own domain) on
  business listings: strong signal nobody set up their IT properly; also a signal they may
  be small or price-sensitive. Record both edges. Check every published email, not just the
  first: practices often list a proper info@ address AND a consumer mailbox on the same
  contact page, and the second one is the signal.
- Legacy tech visible in photos or job listings ("must know" a years-old desktop accounting
  package, OS or environment wording that reads like it was written years ago).

### Incumbent provider check

- Search "[company name] IT support" and check the usual local MSP client pages and
  testimonials. A business publicly happy with a competitor scores lower; a business with no
  visible IT relationship at 40 employees scores higher.

## Part 3: The Desk Score

The msp-sales ICP scoring table assumes a conversation. This rubric is that table restricted
to what a desk researcher can see. Score each candidate 0-100:

| Signal | Points | Desk evidence |
|--------|--------|---------------|
| Headcount in 25-75 | 25 (10-24 or 76-100: 15; outside 10-100: 0 and stop) | Professional network count or triangulated estimate |
| No IT staff found on the professional network | 20 (IT hire posting: 5; IT staff found: 0) | Professional network people search |
| Compliance-heavy vertical | 20 | Vertical classification |
| Growth signals present | 10 | Jobs, press, expansion |
| IT pain clues present | 10 | Website, reviews, listings |
| Local and established (5+ years, community presence) | 10 | State registration date, chamber membership |
| Identifiable decision maker | 5 | Named owner/manager findable (see contact-playbook) |

**Bands:** 70+ green (dossier-worthy), 45-69 yellow (list them, revisit when signals
improve), under 45 red (log and skip; note why).

Unknowns score zero, not a guess. If unknowns are what's keeping a promising business yellow,
say so in the notes column; that is exactly what a first call is for.

**Verify before it matters.** Directory sites (review platforms, health-provider
directories, chamber listings) go stale and contradict each other on staffing, ownership,
and even whether a business is open. For anything that will appear in a dossier or an
outreach draft (names, headcounts, "still in business"), prefer the business's own website
and professional network page, cross-check one second source, and flag conflicts instead of
picking silently.
