# Contact Playbook: Finding the Decision Maker and Planning the Approach

The dossier's job is to answer three questions: who decides, how do we reach them, and what
do we say first. This file covers the first two and hands the third to msp-sales.

---

## Part 1: Finding the Decision Maker

### Who decides, by business size and type

- **Under ~30 employees:** the owner decides, full stop. Find the owner.
- **30-75 employees:** the owner still signs, but an office manager, operations manager, or
  practice manager often owns the problem and champions the deal. Find both; approach the
  problem-owner first when the owner is hard to reach.
- **Professional practices (medical, dental, legal, CPA):** the practice manager or office
  administrator handles vendors; partners/doctors sign. The practice manager is the door.
- **Construction and manufacturing:** often a controller or office manager runs everything
  administrative, including IT. The owner is in the field or on the floor.

### Where to find them

1. **The website:** about/team pages, "meet the owner" blurbs, staff directories.
2. **The professional network:** search the company page, filter people by title (owner,
   president, managing partner, office manager, operations, controller, practice manager).
3. **Your state's business registry:** the registered agent is often the owner at small
   businesses.
4. **Chamber directories and local press:** quotes and award writeups name principals.
5. **Public reviews:** owner responses on review platforms are often signed with a first
   name.

Record the person's name, title, and the source. If no individual is findable, say so; "call
and ask for the office manager" is an honest plan, inventing a name is not.

### Match them to a msp-sales persona

Tag each contact with the closest persona from msp-sales
`references/ideal-customer-profile.md` (Owner/Operator, Office Manager/Operations,
Compliance-Driven Buyer, Tech-Aware Skeptic). The persona sets the tone of the first touch
and which pain points lead. Note the evidence for the match in one line.

## Part 2: Choosing and Sequencing Channels

Recommend a primary channel and a fallback per prospect, never a blast across all four. The
plan should read like advice from someone who knows this specific business.

### Channel selection guide

| Situation | Primary | Why |
|-----------|---------|-----|
| Named owner with findable email | Cold email | Cheapest first touch, easy to personalize, lets them respond on their terms |
| Practice with a front desk (medical, dental, legal) | Call the practice manager | Email to info@ dies at the front desk; the practice manager answers the phone |
| Storefront or office in a walkable business district | Walk-in with a leave-behind | Local trust business; a face beats an email in your local market |
| Owner active on the professional network (posts, comments) | Connect-then-message on the professional network | They live there; a thoughtful comment plus connect outperforms cold email |
| High-value target (70+ score, 50+ seats, compliance vertical) | Direct mail, then call | A physical letter to a named owner gets opened; follow the letter within a week |
| Only generic contact info findable | Call to identify the decision maker, then restart channel selection | First goal is a name, not a pitch |

### Timing by business type

- **Professional offices (CPA, legal, finance):** Tuesday-Thursday, mid-morning. Avoid CPA
  firms February-April 15 entirely; note tax-season-adjacent timing in the plan.
- **Medical and dental:** lunch window or right after close; mornings are patient crush.
- **Construction and trades:** before 8am or after 4pm; the decision maker is on site midday.
- **Manufacturing:** mid-morning or early afternoon, office staff hours.
- **Retail and hospitality:** avoid their rush (mornings for coffee, weekends for retail);
  Tuesday-Wednesday early afternoon is safest.

### Sequencing

Default cadence for a green prospect: primary touch, then a second touch on a different
channel 4-6 business days later, then a third touch 1-2 weeks after that. Three touches
before resting the lead; msp-sales' sequence templates govern the copy for each. The dossier
specifies the sequence; the pipeline tracks execution.

## Part 3: Legal Ground Rules

These are the floor for any recommended approach. When in doubt, flag it for the owner
rather than assuming, and take real legal questions to an attorney licensed in {{STATE}}.

- **Cold email (CAN-SPAM):** B2B cold email is legal in the US. Every email needs
  {{COMPANY_NAME}}'s physical mailing address, a truthful subject line, no misleading
  headers, and honoring of opt-outs within 10 days. One personalized email to a named
  business contact is fine; purchased bulk lists are off-script without the owner's explicit
  decision. Setup decision: if your brand kit does not publish a street address, drafts
  carry a `[mailing address]` placeholder until you designate one (PO box or office); never
  invent an address to fill it.
- **Cold calls (TCPA):** manual calls to business landlines are fine. No robocalls, no
  autodialed calls or texts to cell numbers without consent. Do not recommend texting a
  prospect who has never been in contact.
- **Professional network outreach:** stay within the platform's normal use: personalized
  connection requests and messages from the owner's real profile. No automation tools, no
  scraping, no connect-and-pitch spam; recommend engaging with a prospect's content before
  the request when time allows.
- **Direct mail:** essentially unregulated for B2B; keep the envelope honest (no fake
  "invoice" or "urgent notice" tricks).
- **State consumer privacy laws:** comprehensive state privacy acts generally apply at
  volumes far beyond a small MSP's prospecting scale, but their spirit is the guide:
  business contact data, used respectfully, deleted when stale. Check whether {{STATE}} has
  one and what it covers.
- **Do-not-contact hygiene:** if a prospect says stop, they go on a do-not-contact list in
  the spreadsheet (there is a column) and every future sweep respects it.

## Part 4: What the First Touch Needs From the Dossier

When drafting the first touch (SKILL.md workflow step 6), pull from the dossier:

- The **angle**: the one observed fact that makes this outreach specific ("saw you're hiring
  three people," "noticed the practice just added a second location"). Never fabricate
  familiarity; if research surfaced nothing personal, lead with the vertical pain point.
- The **persona**: sets vocabulary and the pain point that leads, per msp-sales.
- The **channel format**: email under 150 words per msp-sales; call scripts follow the
  msp-sales script structure; professional network messages shorter still (2-3 sentences);
  letters one page maximum.
- **Brand rules** from msp-brand: voice, contact block, no em dashes.

The draft is labeled DRAFT and nothing is ever sent by this skill.
